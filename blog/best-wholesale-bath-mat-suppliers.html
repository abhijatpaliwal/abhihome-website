A low quote from the wrong kind of factory costs more in the end. Below are four suppliers worth a look, each for a different kind of bath mat range, led by Abhi Home®, followed by the checks I would make before committing to any bulk order.

Instead, this list covers the main product lanes a wholesale buyer usually needs to fill: absorbent cotton bath mats and bath rugs for retail and private label, plastic anti-slip mats, firm stone-style mats, and ready stock for hotels. Cotton bath mat constructions and backings explained

Most disagreements between buyers and factories start with a word that meant different things to each side. "Tufted" and "woven" are the two that cause the most trouble, so it is worth being precise in your brief.

Tufted bath mats are made by punching cotton yarn through a base fabric, then locking the loops or cut pile in place with a backing. They are plush and soft underfoot and take raised patterns well, such as cobblestones, stripes and carved designs. The backing does real work here: it holds the pile, adds grip and affects how the mat behaves in the wash.

Woven bath mats are made in one piece on the loom, so the texture is part of the structure rather than a pile on top. They usually dry faster and shed less, and they can be fully reversible. Two mats can look alike in a photo and still be different constructions, which is exactly why your brief should name the construction and your sample approval should confirm it.

Chenille bath rugs use a soft, velvety chenille yarn; ours are 100% cotton chenille. Crochet-edged mats add a hand-crocheted border to a cotton ground and sell well as a decorative upgrade. Reversible mats have no backing at all, which suits buyers who want an all-cotton mat with two usable sides.

Backing choice is the other big decision. Latex and spray latex are the most common choices; hot melt and TPR rubber are the alternatives, each with its own feel, grip and cost. They also behave differently with heat, so match the backing to the care label you plan to print, and test the finished mat through the wash cycle your customers will actually use. Our guide to bath mat constructions goes into more detail.

If you are building a coordinated bath range, decide early how closely the pieces must match. A mat, a contour and a lid cover cut from the same dye lot will match; pieces dyed separately can drift by a shade, which shoppers notice when the set sits together on a shelf. For runners and large bath rugs made to custom sizes, ask how the backing is applied across long lengths and how the edges are finished, because a long rug puts more strain on both than a standard mat does.

US rules to check before you import bath mats

For the US market, three sets of rules come up on almost every bath mat program. None of them is difficult, but each needs to be agreed with the factory before production, not discovered at the port.

  • Flammability of small rugs. Bath mats and small rugs fall under the federal surface flammability standard for small carpets and rugs, 16 CFR Part 1631. It covers rugs with no dimension greater than 6 feet and an area of no more than 24 square feet. A small rug that does not meet the standard must carry a permanent warning label, so ask your supplier how each construction and backing has been tested.
  • Fiber content and origin labeling. The Federal Trade Commission's guide to labeling requirements under the Textile and Wool Acts explains how fiber content, country of origin and the identity of the manufacturer or dealer must appear. The guide also confirms that rug backings are excluded from the fiber content disclosure, so the percentages describe the face of the mat. Agree the exact label text, and who supplies it, at the sampling stage.
  • Cotton claims. If you plan to market a mat as "100% cotton", the FTC's guidance on labeling and advertising cotton products is worth reading, especially for blended yarns and backings.

Certification claims need the same care. If your range will be sold as organic, check the factory's certificate on the Global Organic Textile Standard website, and for tested-for-harmful-substances claims, check the label number against OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100. A certificate must cover the product and the site that makes it, not just the company name. Our article on OEKO-TEX, GOTS and GRS explains what each one proves.

What to put in a wholesale bath mat brief

A clear brief is the cheapest quality control you will ever buy. When every supplier quotes against the same document, the quotes become comparable and the sample approval becomes a real check. This is what I ask buyers to send us, and what I would send any supplier:

  • Size and shape: each size in the set, including any contour, lid cover or runner, with tolerances.
  • Construction: tufted, woven, reversible, chenille or crochet-edged, and loop or cut pile where relevant.
  • Fiber and certification: conventional or organic cotton, recycled content, and which certificates must appear on the documents.
  • Weight target: pile weight or GSM range, because it drives both hand feel and cost.
  • Backing: latex, spray latex, hot melt, TPR rubber or none, and the grip performance you expect.
  • Colors: color standard references and how many colorways per design.
  • Care and labels: the wash and dry instructions you want printed, plus fiber content and origin text.
  • Packaging: header card, belly band, polybag or box, barcode format and carton marks.
  • Shipping basis: FOB, CIF or DDP, and the destination port or warehouse. If you are unsure which to choose, our guide to FOB vs CIF vs DDP explains the trade-offs.

Then ask each supplier to confirm, in writing, the sampling steps, the order quantity and timing for your exact specification, payment milestones and how defects are handled. Our post on bath mat MOQs, lead times and sampling covers what to expect at each stage.

Wholesale pricing depends on material, construction, size, order volume, branding and packing. Ask suppliers to quote the same specification, then compare the unit cost alongside freight, duties where applicable, and the cost of any custom setup. A lower unit quote may not be the lower landed cost. If your sourcing team handles these conversations over video calls, structured negotiation practice, such as the remote programs from Edge Negotiation Group, can help keep the discussion on total terms rather than unit price alone.

Many retailers end up using more than one supplier, and that is usually the right call. A cotton specialist makes the absorbent mats and bath rugs that carry your brand on the shelf, while an in-tub grip mat or a stone-style mat comes from a factory set up for plastics or diatomite. Splitting the range this way keeps each product with a factory that makes it every day, which tends to show up in fewer quality problems and clearer test paperwork.

Pro tip

Keep the approved sample and the signed specification together. Use them as the reference point for production checks and any later quality review.

Frequently asked questions

What should I look for in a wholesale bath mat supplier?

Start with a supplier whose material and construction fit your customers and use case. Then check sample quality, backing, care instructions, customization, certifications and written order terms. Ask for the order quantity and timing for your exact specification. For a private label program, approve the packaging and branding details before production begins.

Is a tufted bath mat better than a woven one?

Neither is better in general. Tufted mats feel plusher and take raised patterns well; woven mats are made in one piece, dry faster and can be reversible. Choose by the feel and care routine your customers expect, and name the construction in your brief so the sample and bulk order match.

Do bath mats need flammability testing for the US?

Yes. Bath mats and other small rugs fall under 16 CFR Part 1631, the federal standard for the surface flammability of small carpets and rugs. Ask your supplier how each construction and backing meets it, and agree any required labeling before production.

How do I compare wholesale bath mat quotes?

Send each supplier the same written specification. Compare unit price with order quantity, customization, packaging, payment terms and shipping basis. Ask whether freight is included and who manages import steps. A quote is only comparable when the product details and delivery terms match.

Which supplier suits a certified private label bath mat program?

We hold BCI, GOTS, OEKO-TEX and GRS certifications and work with flexible MOQs. Ask for the documents that apply to your specific product, destination and order before committing to production.
