Importing bath rugs from India: a buyer's checklist

Why a checklist

Importing bath rugs from India is not complicated, but it has more moving parts than most first-time buyers expect. The rug itself is the easy bit. The work is in the brief, the sampling, the labels, the market rules and the paperwork that has to arrive with the container. Miss one and a good product sits at the port.

This is the checklist we walk new buyers through. It is written from the factory side, so it covers what we need from you and what you should expect from us. One caution before we start: rules change, and your own compliance team or testing lab has the final word for your market. Treat this as a map, not legal advice.

Before you enquire: the brief

The quality of the first quote depends on the quality of the first brief. You do not need a finished tech pack, but these points save a lot of back and forth:

  • Construction. Tufted, chenille, woven, reversible or quick-dry. A photo of something close is fine.
  • Format and sizes. Single rug, set or runner, and the sizes for each market you sell into.
  • Backing. Latex, spray latex, hot melt, TPR rubber or unbacked, or tell us how the rug will be washed and we will suggest one.
  • Colours. Pantone references or physical swatches.
  • Quantities. Per colour and per size. MOQs depend on construction, so real numbers get you a real answer.
  • Certification and claims. If you plan to say organic or recycled on the label, say so now.
  • Target price and delivery window. Both shape the construction we recommend.

Sampling: approve the right things

Our counter-samples typically take 10 to 14 days and pre-production samples around 21 days. Use them well. Approve colour against your standard in daylight, not on a screen. Wash the sample the way your customers will, then check shrinkage, colour and whether the pile stays put. Check that the backing still grips after washing. For runners and large rugs, ask for a full-size sample, because size changes how a rug lies and washes.

Whatever you approve becomes the standard the bulk order is inspected against. Keep the signed sample and the written approval together.

Compliance by market

This is where most delays come from. The headline rules for bath rugs in three of the markets our buyers sell into most:

United States

  • Flammability. Small carpets and rugs, which the federal standard defines as having no dimension over 6 feet and an area of no more than 24 square feet, must meet the surface flammability standard at 16 CFR Part 1631. The definition covers mats within those limits. Larger rugs fall under a separate standard (16 CFR Part 1630). Products covered by these rules need a General Certificate of Conformity.
  • Labelling. Under the Textile Fiber Products Identification Act, rugs and mats need a label showing fibre content, country of origin, and the company name or RN. The FTC guidance notes that the backing of a rug is not counted in the fibre content disclosure.

European Union

  • Fibre labelling. The EU Textile Regulation (1007/2011) covers the textile upper layer of floor coverings where textile makes up at least 80% of that layer by weight, so a cotton bath rug needs a correct fibre composition label.
  • Chemicals. REACH restricts azo dyes that can release listed aromatic amines above 30 mg/kg in textiles that touch the skin for long periods. A bath rug is used barefoot, so this matters. OEKO-TEX® Standard 100 testing is the practical way most buyers cover it.
  • Product safety. Under the General Product Safety Regulation, which has applied since December 2024, products sold in the EU need a responsible economic operator established in the EU, usually the importer or the brand.

United Kingdom

  • Great Britain kept its own version of the textile labelling regulation, so fibre labels are still required. Check current UK product safety guidance with your compliance team, as it is being updated.

Organic or recycled claims need their own proof: a GOTS, OCS or GRS transaction certificate issued for your shipment, not just the factory's scope certificate. Our post on OEKO-TEX, GOTS and GRS explains what each certificate actually proves.

Founder note

The most expensive mistake I see is a label change after production. Care and law labels are sewn in at the factory. If your compliance team signs off the wording before we cut, nobody has to unpick a container of rugs.

The documents that travel with the order

When we ship, these go with the goods or to your broker:

  • Commercial invoice and packing list, matched to your purchase order and carton marks.
  • Bill of lading for sea freight, or airway bill for air.
  • Certificate of origin, needed where you claim preferential duty under a trade agreement.
  • Test reports for your market, from your nominated lab or ours.
  • Transaction certificates for any organic or recycled claims.

Ask your customs broker for their list before the first shipment, not after. Some want extra declarations, and it is easier to prepare them at the factory than at the port.

Incoterms and freight

We quote FOB Mundra or Nhava Sheva as standard, with CIF and DDP on request. FOB suits buyers with their own forwarder and freight rates. DDP suits buyers who want a landed price and fewer moving parts. US buyers can also contract through our US-based trading entity, with goods shipped direct from India. If the terms are new to you, our guide to FOB, CIF and DDP sets out who pays for what.

Packing and labels

Agree the retail packaging and the carton spec in writing: how each rug or set is folded or rolled, the header card or band, barcodes, carton size and marks, and pieces per carton. Care labels and law labels are sewn in at the factory, so their wording must be final before production. Our post on packaging and labelling for export has a fuller checklist.

A realistic timeline

For bath rugs, plan on bulk production of around 60 to 75 days after sample approval, plus 21 to 35 days of ocean transit to most markets. Add sampling time at the front. Working backwards from your in-store date is the single best way to avoid paying for air freight. Our post on bath mat MOQs, lead times and sampling goes through it step by step.

Frequently asked questions

Do bath rugs need flammability testing in the US?

Generally yes. Small carpets and rugs, including mats, with no dimension over 6 feet and an area of no more than 24 square feet fall under the federal surface flammability standard at 16 CFR Part 1631. Larger rugs fall under 16 CFR Part 1630. Confirm the test plan with your lab.

What must a US label on a cotton bath rug show?

Fibre content, country of origin, and the company name or RN number, under the Textile Fiber Products Identification Act. The backing is not counted in the fibre content disclosure.

What documents ship with a bath rug order from India?

Typically a commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, certificate of origin where preferential duty is claimed, test reports for the destination market, and transaction certificates for any organic or recycled claims.

How long does it take to import bath rugs from India?

Plan on roughly 60 to 75 days of bulk production after sample approval, plus 21 to 35 days of ocean transit to most markets, with sampling time added at the front.
