Hotel bedspreads suppliers: a buyer's guide to choosing

By Abhijat Paliwal, founder, Abhi Home®, third-generation Indian home textile manufacturer (since 1965).

A bedspread is the first thing a guest sees when the room door opens, and the first thing housekeeping strips when they leave. That double life is why choosing between hotel bedspreads suppliers is harder than it looks: the piece has to photograph well for the brand, survive commercial laundry for years, and come back in the same color on every reorder. I am Abhijat Paliwal, and at Abhi Home® we have made home textiles in India since 1965, including quilted bedspreads and coverlets as OEM / private label production under the buyer's brand. This guide explains how a hotel purchasing team can judge a supplier: constructions, laundry, sizing, US labels, certifications, the brief and the quotes.

What hotels mean by a bedspread

The word means different things in different purchasing offices, so settle it before you ask anyone for a price. In our trade, a bedspread is a top layer that covers the whole bed and usually hangs over the sides. A quilted bedspread has a face fabric, a fill and a back held together by stitching. A coverlet is lighter and often sits above the mattress line without a long drop. A quilt is the quilted layer itself, sold flat or as part of a set with shams. A bed runner is a narrow band across the foot of the bed that adds color without covering it.

A good supplier will ask which of these you mean, because each one changes the fabric, the weight, the size and the price. Our quilt and bedspread buying guide covers the vocabulary and the fills in more depth.

Where a bedspread fits in a hotel top of bed

Most hotel groups now choose between two top of bed approaches. One is a white duvet with a runner or a folded throw for color. The other is a bedspread or coverlet that carries the room's color and pattern on its own. Many properties mix the two: duvets in standard rooms, a quilted bedspread or coverlet in suites, resorts and boutique rooms where the bed is the main design statement.

The choice matters to the supplier conversation. A white duvet program is mostly about fabric and laundry. A bedspread program is about design, color and repeatability as well, so you need a maker that can hold a color across many orders, not only deliver one good shipment.

Constructions that suit hotel rooms

We make quilts, quilted bedspreads and coverlets in cotton, linen and velvet. Each construction answers a different room and a different laundry plan.

Box and grid quilted bedspreads

Straight line quilting in squares or channels is clean and contemporary, and it suits stripes, solids and crinkled cotton. It is the easiest construction to repeat exactly across a large room count, which is why I suggest it for a first hotel program.

Engineered quilting

An engineered pattern, such as diamonds or tonal florals, is programmed on the quilting machine and repeated identically on every piece. It gives a richer surface than a plain grid while staying consistent from the first room to the last.

Matelassé and seersucker coverlets

Coverlets are lighter and layerable. A matelassé coverlet has a raised jacquard weave surface, light enough to layer and substantial enough to use alone, and it suits boutique hospitality. A seersucker coverlet has a crinkled stripe texture and works for coastal and contemporary rooms.

Hand kantha coverlets

Kantha is the Indian running stitch, worked by hand across the whole piece, so every coverlet has a slightly different natural texture. It is made to order and fits boutique hotels and suites rather than a large standard room count. Because we run hand and machine quilting under one compliance scope, a property can order a hand stitched coverlet for suites and an engineered quilt for standard rooms in the same program.

Velvet quilts and patchwork are also possible for statement suites, though they need more care in laundry. The full range sits on our bedding collection page.

Commercial laundry is the real test

A retail bedspread may be washed a few times a year. A hotel bedspread goes through industrial washers and dryers far more often, at higher temperatures and with stronger chemistry. That is where weak programs fail: shrinkage that pulls the drop above the mattress line, quilting that puckers, colors that fade unevenly, bindings that split at the corners.

Four things in the specification decide how a bedspread survives laundry:

  • Fabric and color. Solid whites and light neutrals hide wear well. Deep colors and prints need confirmed colorfastness under the laundry's actual process.
  • Fill and stitch density. The fill type and weight decide warmth and loft, and also how the stitch sits after washing. Wider stitch spacing lets the fill migrate.
  • Edge finish. Self binding, contrast binding, piping or scalloped edges all behave differently. Corners take the most stress.
  • Wash and shrinkage targets. Write the targets into the brief and have the approved sample washed and measured before bulk. Ask your laundry partner for its wash program so the test reflects real use.

Sizing bedspreads for every bed type

Hotel beds are rarely all one size, and mattress depths have grown with toppers and pillow tops. Measure the actual mattress top and depth for each bed type in the property, including any topper, before you set a size. Then decide the drop: a full bedspread that reaches near the floor, a coverlet that ends below the mattress line, or a runner that sits across the foot.

We make bedspreads in standard sizes for your market, oversized drops and fully custom dimensions, with matching shams where the design calls for them. For a hotel, I suggest one SKU per bed type with the same construction and color, so housekeeping is never matching the wrong piece to the wrong bed. Remember that shrinkage targets must be agreed before you lock the cut size, otherwise the drop changes after the first few washes.

US labels, customs and fire specifications

For hotels in the United States, three rule sets matter.

Labels. Bedspreads and quilts are covered as bedding under the Textile Fiber Products Identification Act rules in 16 CFR Part 303. The FTC's guide Threading Your Way Through the Labeling Requirements lists bedspreads among the covered products and explains that labels must show fiber content, country of origin and the identity of the manufacturer or the business marketing the item, for example by an RN. Your label goes on in our factory, so the artwork and wording should be approved with the sample.

Customs. Bedspreads are generally classified under heading 6304, other furnishing articles, of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States, and the exact line depends on fiber, on whether the piece is knitted or woven, and on trims such as embroidery, piping or appliqué. Confirm the line with your customs broker before you finalize the design, because a trim can move it.

Fire specifications. The federal flammability standard in 16 CFR Part 1632 covers mattresses and mattress pads, not bedspreads. Hotel groups, local codes and contracts can still set their own fire requirements for top of bed textiles. If your group has one, put the exact standard in the brief so the fabric, fill and any finish are chosen to meet it from the start.

Certifications and the documents to ask for

Certifications are only useful when they cover the product you are buying. OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100 is our standard for bedding; the OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100 label means the textile, down to every thread and accessory, has been tested for harmful substances. GOTS certified organic cotton is available on request, GRS recycled options suit groups with recycled content targets, and we are a BCI member for cotton sourcing.

Ask any supplier for the certificate itself, check that its scope covers the product type, and for GOTS or GRS goods ask for the chain of custody documents that travel with each shipment. Our comparison of OEKO-TEX, GOTS and GRS explains what each one proves.

How to choose between hotel bedspreads suppliers

There are three kinds of supplier in this market. Hospitality distributors hold finished stock and deliver fast, but you take their designs and sizes. Design studios create a look and subcontract the making. Manufacturers make the piece to your specification and put your label on it. Abhi Home® works only as a private label manufacturer, for hospitality groups, importers, wholesalers and retail chains.

Whichever route you take, these questions separate a reliable partner from a risky one:

  1. Do they make the construction you need? Ask to see the quilting, weaving or hand work done in their own facility, not only photos.
  2. Will they wash test your sample? A supplier that measures the sample after washing is thinking about your laundry, not only the showroom.
  3. Can they repeat the color? Ask how they hold shade from one order to the next, and whether they keep approved standards on file for reorders.
  4. Do they document compliance? Certificates, test reports and chain of custody papers should come without chasing.
  5. Will they label and pack for you? Hotel orders often ship by property or by floor, so packing and carton marks belong in the plan.
  6. Are the minimums realistic? We keep MOQs flexible and lowest at sampling stage. Our note on how MOQs work explains what drives them.

What to put in a hotel bedspread brief

A clear brief shortens sampling and makes quotes comparable. These are the points we confirm with every buyer before the first sample is cut:

  • Face and back fabric: cotton, linen, velvet or organic cotton, with weave, print or color and fabric weight
  • Fill type and weight, or no fill for a coverlet
  • Quilting pattern and stitch spacing: box, channel, diamond, engineered or hand kantha
  • Size and drop for each bed type, measured from the actual mattresses
  • Set contents: bedspread only, or with shams, runners or cushion covers
  • Edge finish: self binding, contrast binding, piping or scalloped
  • Wash and shrinkage targets, your laundry program and any fire specification
  • Care label, brand label and packing by property

Our guide to private label development walks through the full process, and packaging and labeling for export covers the cartons.

How to compare bedspread quotes

Two quotes for a bedspread can look alike and describe different products. Before comparing prices, line them up on the same fabric, fill weight, stitch spacing, size, edge finish and packing. Check the trade term too: a price ex factory, FOB, CIF or delivered duty paid includes very different costs, as our guide to FOB, CIF and DDP explains. Then weigh what the price buys over the life of the bedspread: a piece that holds its size and color through laundry costs less per night than a cheaper one replaced early.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a hotel bedspread and a coverlet?

A bedspread usually covers the whole bed with a long drop and is often quilted with a fill. A coverlet is lighter, often unfilled, and usually ends below the mattress line. Both can carry the room's color and pattern.

Which bedspread construction is best for a hotel?

For a large room count, box, grid or engineered quilting is the easiest to repeat exactly and to launder. Matelassé coverlets suit boutique rooms, and hand kantha is best kept for suites because each piece is made by hand.

What labels does an imported hotel bedspread need in the US?

Under the FTC textile rules, bedspreads need a label with fiber content by percentage, country of origin and the identity of the manufacturer or marketer, such as an RN. Your hotel group or brand may add its own care and logo labels.

Yes. Abhi Home® is a private label manufacturer only, so every bedspread program runs under your brand, care label, specification and packing. OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100 is our standard for bedding, with GOTS and GRS options on request.
